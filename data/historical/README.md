# Historical counts

[`historical_ois_evidence.csv`](historical_ois_evidence.csv) logs every pre-series count found for the hand-built panel: one row per agency-year (or per source, where a count was found but not used), with its source, page or database ID, a verbatim quote, and notes. Rows with `use_in_panel = yes` feed `n_nix`. Counts of incidents, or of everyone shot rather than people killed, are logged but kept out of the panel. The search tools are in `tools/historical_ois/`.

## Newspaper reconstructions

For twenty-two agencies, years missing from the hand-built series come from local newspapers, searched through NewsBank's Access World News. For Dallas, they fill the 1992–2002 gap between Geller and Scott and the city's open data; for Austin, 2000–2007 as well.

**What counts.** A death counts when the paper reports that an on-duty officer of that agency shot and killed someone. Off-duty officers (including those working extra jobs), other agencies at the same scene, and suicides during standoffs are left out.

**How each year was searched.** Several ways: full-text searches pairing kill phrases with the agency's name, headline searches, and stories about grand jury or district attorney rulings. Each window runs to January 15 of the next year. Every case and every exclusion is logged with its article and database ID. Where the paper printed its own list, running count, or year-end figure, the reconstruction is checked against it.

**Limits.** The method depends on finding every story, so it can undercount, especially where the archive is thin; read the case-by-case years as floors. Chicago and Los Angeles are different: their newspaper years are department counts the papers printed, not case-by-case rebuilds.

The SHR column is the FBI's count of people killed by the agency's officers with a firearm over the same years. It has no on/off-duty field, so it includes some off-duty killings that are left out here.
